Build priority: most clients covered × closest to what we can do

  1. Wave 1
    M1 + M2 + the baseline part of M6

    Covers the largest pool — mid-market companies that bought licenses and saw nothing happen. No development skills needed. The baseline form must go live together with M2; miss that window and it can't be recovered.

  2. Wave 2
    M3 + M4

    The entry to every deep engagement. The scenario library, scoring definitions and role cards accumulate across clients — start early.

  3. Wave 3
    M5 light → medium tier

    Light tier ships now. For medium, build our own demo agent and work through knowledge curation and citation accuracy before committing to timelines. Heavy tier not yet.

  4. Gap
    M7

    Deliver the assessment ourselves after one practice run on Microsoft's oversharing blueprint; label taxonomy, DLP and permission remediation via partners short-term, hires mid-term. Be honest with regulated clients about the boundary.

M1

Usage Baseline Audit

Deliverable
InLicense assignments + M365 usage dataOutFour-tier segmentation, dead-zone list, power-user list
What it is

Pull the client's license assignment list and M365/Copilot usage data and place everyone into four tiers: not licensed / licensed but never used / low-frequency / heavy use. Cross-tabulate department × app to find dead zones where a whole department is at zero. Flag permission and oversharing risks along the way — these feed M7.

How

Data comes from the M365 admin center usage reports, Copilot usage analytics and the license list. Three tasks: segment, find dead zones, interview 3–5 power users (these interviews double as seeds for M3 — do them once).

Assets to build
  • Data request checklist — which reports the client must provide, where to export them, what permissions are needed. Without it every engagement starts from scratch, and permissions often take two weeks
  • Tier definitions — what counts as low-frequency or heavy must be uniform across the company, or two consultants on the same client reach two conclusions
  • Diagnostic report template — four fixed charts: tier funnel, department heatmap, app distribution, dead-zone list
  • Power-user interview guide — 15–20 minutes; ask about concrete usage, not how they feel about it
People

Can read M365 usage reports and build pivot tables. No development skills needed.

Traps
  • Client can't provide data or lacks permissions — the module stallsPut the permission request in the kick-off, not at the start of work
  • Trusting the overall active rate: 85% "active" may mean once a monthAlways look at the frequency distribution
  • Using the report to shame client IT — they usually did their bestFrame it as "let's find where the value is together"
How others do it 3 comparisons

Microsoft internally300,000+ employees

Cohorts cut along "internal organization × region", each treated as its own organization with its own champions and leaders. Data from admin center reports, Copilot Dashboard and Viva Insights, paired with Viva Pulse / Glint surveys. The sales-team retrospective: usage data alone isn't enough — you need surveys and in-the-moment feedback to see where people are stuck.

UK Dept for Business and Trade (DBT)1,000 licenses

Two-thirds used it at least weekly, but only 30% daily; across 63 working days, 1.14 actions per user per day; Loop under 1%, OneNote 3%, PowerPoint / Excel peaking at 7%.

Practical365 methodUsage reports + unified audit log

Score each user: (days since last use ÷ apps used) − (total interactions ÷ 10); ≥30 flags low utilization. Someone who uses Copilot heavily but only in Outlook isn't misread as inactive.

What it means for us
DBT is the public example of "85% active might mean once a month" — the frequency and app distribution charts are non-negotiable. Start tier definitions from the Practical365 formula and tune thresholds per client. Microsoft pairs data with listening — the same approach as our "segmentation + 3–5 power-user interviews".
M2

Tiered Enablement

Deliverable
InTier lists from M1OutTiered training, champion network, scenario cards
What it is

Coach each M1 tier separately. The tiers have completely different goals — lumping them together is exactly why the previous round failed.

How
TierGoalApproach
Never usedA first successful experience60–90 minutes; teach one scenario closest to their job, do it live, leave with one working prompt. No feature tour
Low-frequencyFrom occasional to dailyGroup by role; 3–5 high-frequency scenarios + prompt patterns; teach "how to break down your own work and which part can go to AI"
Heavy useReplicate themNot training — interviewing. Distill what they figured out into assets, then invite them to be champions
Assets to build
  • Role scenario cards — the most reusable asset. One each for project manager, financial analyst, HR, sales, admin, legal: what the role repeats daily, matching prompts, before/after. Reused across clients — by client 5 they should cover most roles
  • Three tiered course packs — every session tied to a real scenario; no "feature introduction" sessions
  • Prompt pattern library — patterns, not a giant prompt dump: summarize, draft, rewrite, analyze, Q&A, each with structure and examples
  • Champion handbook — what champions do each month, who to go to, how to escalate
  • Post-training feedback form — two weeks after training: useful or not, used for what, stuck where. Also a data source for M6
People

At least one person who can rewrite prompts on the spot and explain why (button-clickers won't hold up); one person who can run a champion community.

Traps
  • One-size-fits-all trainingRepeats the client's last failure
  • No feedback collection after trainingNobody knows if it worked, and M6 has no data
  • Picking senior champions who don't do the workPick people who actually use it, regardless of seniority
How others do it 4 comparisons

KOHLER10,000+ employees

Tiered by business segment, entry-level to advanced, framed as "business skilling": dissect processes and turn them into AI workflows — not feature training. 98% adoption in 40 days against a goal of 95% in 9 months. See case library

Australian Government trial7,600+ staff, 60+ agencies

Of those who received 3 or more forms of training, 75% felt confident — 28 percentage points higher than those with only one.

Microsoft Copilot Champs~10,000 people

Recruits generalists who "know a little about a lot", regardless of seniority, and finds early adopters via usage surveys. Onboarding: welcome email + baseline training + completion badge + kick-off + baseline poll. Ongoing: Viva Engage community, Teams channel, train-the-trainer, community calls, shout-outs. Some champions run 1–2 demos a week. "It takes just one magical use case to turn a skeptic into a champion." Microsoft also claims 30% more active days for community participants (vendor internal study).

Microsoft sales-team retrospective

Role-based immersion clearly beat generic guidance; sustained use comes from habit, not enthusiasm — embed simple prompts into existing workflows.

What it means for us
The Australian data is hard evidence that one training session isn't enough. Scenario cards, champion Q&A and feedback collection all count as touchpoints — price for multiple touchpoints. Microsoft's champion onboarding can go almost directly into our handbook. "One magical use case" is the same judgment as teaching the never-used tier just one scenario.
M3

Scenario Discovery

Deliverable
InThe client's actual workOutStructured list of candidate scenarios
What it is

Collect candidate scenarios from real work and produce a structured list that can be scored in the next step.

How · four sources, ranked by return
  1. Mining power users — highest return; the scenarios are already validated by real work
  2. Role workshops — group by work pattern (not department) and list the work that repeats every week
  3. High-frequency question analysis — IT tickets, repeated internal questions, "how do I…" emails. PwC's 90% how-to tickets show this is a gold mine
  4. Leadership pain-point interviews — adds the strategic view, but never on its own; leaders' pain points are often not the front line's

Every scenario records the same fields: who does it, what the task is, how often, how long it takes now, where the input lives, what the output is, who reviews it.

Assets to build
  • Scenario capture form — fixed fields; missing fields mean it can't be scored later
  • Role workshop agenda + whiteboard template
  • Cross-client scenario library — the core of how the practice gets stronger. By client 5, most of a new client's scenarios should be familiar, cutting discovery from days to hours
People

A workshop facilitator who can get the front line talking and keep leadership from dominating.

Traps
  • Starting with "what features do you want"Guaranteed failure
  • Unstructured notesM4 can't happen
  • Scenarios at the grain of "write reports"Needs to be "every Monday, explain last week's sales anomalies to the regional manager"
How others do it 4 comparisons

Microsoft role-scenario playbook

Ready (leadership alignment, pick priority roles) → Engage (sessions with that role to uncover use cases) → Deliver (hero scenarios with value articulated) → Share (socialize company-wide). Roles first, then dig — no company-wide dragnet.

Copilot Scenario LibraryMicrosoft, public and free

Organized by department (legal, operations, communications, IT…). Each task: action-oriented title (e.g. "Draft a shift summary") + how + which app + benefit, with KPIs on the page.

Grupo BimboGlobal audit team, 100+ auditors

Scenarios came from an internal hackathon where auditors raised their own pain points. Those people became key users in development and testing — which is why the solution fit real work and earned trust early.

Accenture / KPMG

Both did persona research: Accenture interviewed developers, testers, scrum masters and product owners; KPMG defined 28 high-value personas and wrote 88 Day-in-the-Life guides for a mining client. See case library

What it means for us
The Scenario Library is a cold-start seed for our cross-client library, but it stops at generic tasks — no frequency, current time, input location or reviewer. Those are exactly the fields M4 scores on, and the difference between our capture form and theirs. Grupo Bimbo shows the people who articulate pain best become the champions and acceptance users — put them on the candidate list during the workshop.
M4

Scenario Assessment & Selection

Deliverable
InScenario listOutPriority ranking; do / don't / defer
What it is

Score and rank scenarios into four explicit groups: do now / queue / fix data or governance first / don't do. "Don't do" must be said out loud with reasons — that is part of the consulting value, not a cop-out.

How · six-dimension scoring
DimensionQuestion
Business valueDoes it affect cost, revenue, speed, quality or compliance?
FrequencyDaily / weekly / monthly / occasional
Copilot fitIs it information-, writing-, meeting- or analysis-heavy?
Data readinessIs the content accessible, structured, correctly permissioned? — most often skipped, most likely to blow up
Risk levelCould errors cause legal, security or customer harm? Can human review catch them?
User willingnessWill this group actually change how they work?
Assets to build
  • Scoring sheet with weights and definitions — without shared definitions, consultants score differently and the client notices
  • Data readiness checklist — where content lives, format, permissions, update frequency, maintainer
  • Implementation tier decision tree — when to go light / medium / heavy
People

Technical feasibility judgment + business value judgment. Both must be in the room; missing either leads to wrong picks.

Traps
  • Letting the client score — everything scores highWe score, then align definitions with the client
  • Skipping data readinessThe number one reason projects blow up midway
  • Too many in the first batch1–3
How others do it 3 comparisons

Microsoft BXT frameworkUse-case evaluation for partners

Three areas × three components, each 1–5: Business (strategy alignment / business value / change timeframe), Experience (key personas / value proposition / change resistance), Technology (implementation risk / safeguards / AI fit). Rolled into two axes — strategic business impact and executional fit — landing in Shelve / Research / Incubate / Accelerate to MVP. Recommends scoring with a wider group for buy-in.

Microsoft Cloud Adoption Framework

Judge feasibility by AI maturity first (the lowest level can already do any Copilot solution); make the first proof of concept internal and non-customer-facing; Copilot returns value in "days to weeks"; add 20–30% buffer to estimates.

Public "poor-fit zones"

  • UK DBT: Excel analysis with Copilot was slower and worse quality; PowerPoint 7+ minutes faster but lower quality and accuracy; 22% encountered hallucinations
  • Australian Treasury: nearly two-thirds found it fit for basic admin, limited value for complex work
  • Australian Government trial: poor Excel functionality, Outlook access issues; verifying and editing output offset some savings
What it means for us
BXT maps onto our six dimensions ("change resistance" ≈ "user willingness") but doesn't break out data readiness — we're right to. The four-quadrant chart works directly as the client "do / don't" slide. Stakeholder participation belongs at the alignment step; we still score first. Write the poor-fit zones into the "Copilot fit" definitions: Excel analysis, accuracy-critical finished decks, complex professional judgment — a low score should rest on public evidence.
M5

Scenario Implementation

Light: yesMedium: practise firstHeavy: not yet
InSelected scenariosOutSomething usable + instructions + a named owner
How · three tiers, start light by default
TierContentsDuration
LightPrompt templates + document templates + process tweaks + quality checklist; stays in the standard M365 interface1–2 weeks
MediumCopilot Studio agent + knowledge grounding + traceable citations, published to Teams4–8 weeks
HeavyBusiness system integration, two-way actions, exception handling and human fallbackLonger, higher risk

Most scenarios should stop at the light tier. The real work in medium is knowledge curation and citation accuracy, not building the agent — Dunaway's citation accuracy started around 70% and took dedicated engineering to fix.

Assets to build
  • Prompt and document template libraries (organized by scenario type)
  • Agent reference architecture — borrow Dunaway's: knowledge manifest → automated ingestion → single source of truth → search index → citation enhancement
  • Knowledge-source curation method — how to inventory, clean, and who maintains it. Price it as its own line; don't hide it
  • Quality checklist — is output correct, are citations accurate, when must it escalate to a human
  • Acceptance criteria — what counts as done
People

Light: a consultant is enough. Medium: Copilot Studio + retrieval/knowledge-base skills (internal practice run first). Heavy: integration developers — not taking these on yet.

Traps
  • Skipping light, going straight to mediumBurns budget before value is proven
  • Hiding knowledge curation effort in the quoteGuaranteed blow-up midway
  • Making it fully automatedProfessional judgment stays with people
How others do it 4 comparisons

Grupo BimboAudit · what medium tier looks like

Two Copilot Studio agents: Comatrix drafts risk and control matrices from global procedures; Audit Assist answers methodology questions and retrieves templates. Knowledge source: SharePoint content the QA team already maintained, only separated into procedures, templates and standards. Deterministic orchestration; iterating on system prompts was the hardest part; auditors still review output. Planning time down ~20%; matrices from ~2 days to under half a day. Yet by early 2026 only 50–60% of auditors were active users.

DunawayEngineering · medium leaning heavy

Built its own pipeline: manifest of 200+ regulatory sources → Power Automate ingestion → SharePoint → Azure AI Search → citation-accuracy layer; citation accuracy started around 70%. See case library

City of MontréalWebsite agent, 40,000+ pages

After launch, used Copilot Agent Kit conversation KPIs to filter low-satisfaction conversations, checked each for missing content or misrouted topics, and fed fixes back into site content and agent design.

Microsoft agent tiers

Retrieval agents (anyone can build) / knowledge + action agents (with connectors) / workflow automation agents; enterprise agents go through the same reviews and lifecycle as regular apps.

What it means for us
Bimbo and Dunaway are the two ends of medium tier: knowledge already maintained → connect SharePoint, short timeline; knowledge scattered → build a pipeline, noticeably longer and pricier (our judgment). The first question in the tier decision tree: "is anyone maintaining this knowledge today?" Bimbo's 50–60% is a reminder that a working agent isn't a used agent — medium-tier delivery must carry M2 activities. Low-score conversation review goes into the owner's monthly duties.
M6

ROI Measurement & Review

Lacks baseline discipline
InBaseline + usage dataOutValue report, next-round backlog
How · baseline → measure → review
  • Capture the baseline before any work starts. This is the make-or-break point; a baseline reconstructed afterwards relies on memory, and neither we nor the client can trust it
  • Three metric layers: adoption (active, frequency, retention) → behavior (which scenarios, which apps) → outcomes (time per task, cycle time, rework rate, satisfaction)
  • Measurement window: reference KPMG's 28 days
  • Time × labor cost = capacity value, clearly labeled "capacity created", not cash savings
  • Hold the review with the client: what worked, what didn't, why, what's next
Assets to build
  • Baseline capture form — filled in for every scenario before work starts; no form, no start
  • Metric definitions — what counts as active, what counts as a successful use
  • Value report template — three layers + estimate labeling + next-round recommendations
  • ROI calculator — frequency, headcount, before/after time, cost rate in; capacity value out, with disclaimer language added automatically so nobody overstates verbally
People

Someone who can do data analysis; someone who can present numbers honestly to client executives — including numbers that don't look good.

Traps
  • No baselineThe whole module is void
  • Reporting only usageSelf-deception
  • Presenting estimates as factThere won't be a next round
  • Not reporting failed scenariosLoses credibility. Failure analysis is the best renewal argument
How others do it 4 UK departments + 2

Same product, same period, four UK government departments each evaluated it — the more rigorous the method, the smaller the number:

WhoScaleMethodConclusion (as stated)
GDS · Government Digital Service20,000 people, 12 depts, 3 monthsSelf-reported surveys + usage data, no control group26 min/day saved; project delivery 35+, tax roles 11; 82% don't want to go back
HMRC · Revenue & Customs3,000 random + 500 volunteerRandomized cohort; self-reports discounted ~20%~60 min/week (2–3% of the week); ~£50m/year "capacity" projected at 50,000 licenses
DWP · Work & Pensions3,549 people1,716 users vs 2,535 non-users + 19 interviews19 min/day; information search most (26), meeting transcription least (9)
DBT · Business & Trade1,000 licensesControl group + observed task timing + diaries + interviews72% satisfied; no robust evidence time savings became productivity

After the GDS trial, 9 of 12 departments renewed and seats grew to 31,000 — imperfect numbers didn't stop renewal.

Microsoft sales-team retrospective

Pair measurement with listening: usage data says whether it's used; surveys and in-the-moment feedback say where people are stuck.

Accenture

Beyond usage, asked "would you deeply miss it?" (84% yes) to measure entrenchment. See case library

What it means for us
26 minutes → 19 minutes → no robust evidence: the strongest table for explaining why the baseline comes first and why our numbers are more conservative than vendor material — put it on the methodology page of the value report. Three practices to borrow:
  1. HMRC's discount on self-reports → default discount factor in the ROI calculator
  2. HMRC's "capacity" wording → consistent with "capacity created, not cash savings"
  3. DWP's users vs non-users → unlicensed staff in the same department are a ready-made comparison group
DBT shows that reporting satisfaction alone is self-deception.
M7

Governance & Risk

Assessment: after one practice runRemediation: skills gap
InCurrent data and permissionsOutGovernance pack, control rulesWhenUp front for regulated clients; before M5 for everyone else
How
  • Oversharing assessment — the most common failure point: Copilot respects permissions, but surfaces content people "technically had access to but long forgot"
  • Data classification and sensitivity labels
  • Human review rules: which outputs need a person, what must never go out as-is
  • Acceptable use policy: what's allowed, what isn't
  • Operational ownership: who approves new scenarios, maintains templates, monitors value, reclaims licenses
Assets to build
  • Oversharing assessment checklist
  • Governance recommendation pack template — licensing policy, access reviews, data protection, champion duties, feedback and escalation processes, measurement cadence
  • Human review rules template (tiered by risk level)
People

Our clearest capability gap. Needs Purview / Defender / information protection skills. The assessment can follow Microsoft's blueprint after an internal practice run; label taxonomy, DLP and permission remediation need a hire or a partner — don't force it.

Traps
  • Treating it as a wrap-up stepRegulated clients stall at the last mile. Loyens & Loeff is the counterexample: governance first, 1,600 people in two months
  • Producing a document onlyGovernance must land as concrete rules and named people
How others do it 5 comparisons

Gartner surveyJune 2024, 132 IT leaders

40% delayed rollout 3+ months over oversharing concerns; 57% limited rollout to "low-risk or trusted" users; only 6% had finished pilots and planned large-scale deployment.

Microsoft oversharing blueprintOfficial, public

Export the top 100 most-visited sites, run SharePoint Advanced Management permission reports + Purview DSPM for AI assessment; enable Restricted SharePoint Search on selected sites, disable "Everyone except external users" tenant-wide; turn on Purview Audit, Communication Compliance and DLP simulation. These capabilities are included in the Copilot license.

Microsoft internal governance

Four label tiers, max 5 parent + 5 sub-labels; new sites default to private, no guests, files inherit container labels; owners re-attest every 6 months or the container is deleted (recoverable); scans for "Highly Confidential" shared with 5,000+ people. Lesson: build governance into defaults, don't rely on training.

Governance as adoption barrierHMRC · Australia

HMRC: 46% of non-users cited security and privacy concerns as the main reason. Australia's trial and Treasury: unclear data security, information management and legal rules directly limited usage scope and output quality.

Loyens & Loeff / KOHLER

One put governance first and rolled out 1,600 people in two months; the other moved from "security first" to "lead with yes", with governance as a quiet enabler. See case library

What it means for us
  1. Assess ourselves, partner for remediation. "Top-100 site permission report + DSPM assessment + risk list" is deliverable after one practice run; the real gap is label taxonomy, DLP and remediation.
  2. Gartner's 40% / 57% are ready-made numbers for "why M7 runs before M5".
  3. HMRC's 46%: without clear rules on what data can go into Copilot, the front line won't use it. Write the acceptable use policy on one page and put it into M2 training.
